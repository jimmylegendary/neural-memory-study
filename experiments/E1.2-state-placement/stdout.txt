E1.2 STATE-PLACEMENT DSE — where to put the per-token fast-weight state at TTT decode.
A decode step reads S + writes S back (RMW). S is FIXED-size (independent of context) => on-chip
residency is a designable choice, unlike the context-growing KV cache.

Q1) Rank memory devices for a 134 MB per-layer state RMW step (epilogue 3 MAC/elem):
  HBM3 3.35TB/s 7pJ (baseline)       traffic    268.4 MB | energy    1919.3 uJ |      80.1 us (memory)  [fits on-die]
  on-die SRAM 10TB/s 0.4pJ 50MB      traffic    268.4 MB | energy     147.6 uJ |      26.8 us (memory)  [SPILLS to HBM]
  sw-scratchpad 60TB/s 0.9pJ 256MB   traffic    268.4 MB | energy     281.9 uJ |       5.4 us (compute)  [fits on-die]
  PIM in-bank 8.2TB/s 3.9pJ          traffic    268.4 MB | energy    1167.7 uJ |     167.8 us (compute)  [fits on-die]
     -> lowest ENERGY: on-die SRAM 10TB/s 0.4pJ 50MB  |  lowest TIME: sw-scratchpad 60TB/s 0.9pJ 256MB
     -> NOTE: the 50 MB L2 posts the lowest roofline energy but a 134 MB state SPILLS out of it
        (the flat-twin cost path does not enforce residency — that is Q2's job); the REALIZABLE
        winner that actually holds S is the 268 MB scratchpad (both fits AND lowest time).
     -> on-chip residency wins energy AND time by ~1 order of magnitude over HBM3 when S fits.

Decision surface (pre-silicon): the TTT/neural-memory state is FIXED-size, so on-chip residency
is a real design knob — but a 268 MB scratchpad holds only ~2 layers of a 1.3B state and no
whole-model state past the smallest (170M) config, so placement is per-layer/streamed. PIM buys
movement energy at the cost of compute latency (fine at TTT's ~3 MAC/elem). This is the memory-
centric half of the pair thesis: decode state management is a placement/residency problem.
