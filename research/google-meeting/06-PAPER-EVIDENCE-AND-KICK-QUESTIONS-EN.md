# Google Meeting: Paper-Grounded Problems, Open Questions, and Six Kick Questions

**Purpose.** This is the single English evidence appendix for the Google–Samsung SAIT discussion on long-context memory, stateful serving, continual learning, and sleep-time compute. It consolidates the paper-grounded problem statements and open issues behind the six kick questions.

**Evidence boundary.** The “Reference passage” column uses faithful, compact paraphrases rather than verbatim quotations. `PDF p.` is the 1-based physical page shown by a PDF viewer; `Lx–Ly` is the stable line range in the repository’s plain-text extraction. The local text and PDF are the controlling artifacts for locators.

| Mark | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **D-P** | Direct problem statement made by the paper |
| **D-O** | Author-stated discussion point, limitation, unresolved issue, or future work |
| **D-R** | Direct result or design constraint that motivates a question |
| **I-Q** | SAIT question inferred from a paper result or an explicitly bounded scope; not an author claim |

## 3. Six English kick questions

These are **SAIT-authored meeting questions**, not questions quoted from the papers. The evidence anchors show why each question is legitimate and where the papers stop short of answering it.

| ID | Research axis | English kick question | Primary evidence anchors |
|---|---|---|---|
| **KQ-01** | Long-context sufficiency and test-time scaling | **We see the core long-context issue as preserving a state that is sufficient for downstream reasoning, not merely increasing the amount of retrievable history. ## 4. Claim boundaries for the meeting

| Do not claim | Defensible formulation |
|---|---|
| “Memory Caching solves prefix caching for recurrent models.” | MC demonstrates **within-sequence** state checkpoints and query-time aggregation. Cross-request identity, compatibility, security, and reuse rate remain open systems questions. |
| “NSTM solves stateful LLM serving.” | NSTM provides evidence for update/apply frequency separation, immutable repeated reads, finite-capacity pressure, and architecture-dependent state merging in online video. Transferring those results to multi-user LLM serving is an SAIT hypothesis. |
| “HOPE or Sleep solves lifelong continual learning.” | HOPE reduces forgetting in studied tasks and explicitly says the general problem is unsolved. Sleep supplies a consolidation-and-dreaming proof of concept but not an infinite-horizon capacity, eviction, rollback, or scaling law. |
| “A larger memory state is sufficient for reasoning.” | The papers show capacity/recall gains and persistent-state mechanisms. None provides a necessary-and-sufficient test for compositional-reasoning sufficiency; that gap is the point of KQ-01. |
| “Sleep-time compute already has a scaling law.” | The papers identify cadence, consolidation depth, data constraints, capacity activation, and repeated-transfer cost. KQ-06 asks whether these can be turned into a predictive law. |
